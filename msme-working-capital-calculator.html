What does the working capital gap mean?

This simplified tool adds stock, receivables and other operating current assets, then deducts trade creditors and other spontaneous current liabilities. The result represents the net operating funding requirement before considering borrower contribution, existing bank borrowings and other adjustments.

Operating working capital gap = Operating current assets − Spontaneous operating current liabilities

The illustrative finance after margin applies your chosen margin to a positive gap. It is not a bank sanction amount, a drawing-power calculation or a representation of any particular bank’s MPBF method.

Example for an MSME

With stock ₹35 lakh, receivables ₹28 lakh and other operating assets ₹2 lakh, total operating assets are ₹65 lakh. Deducting ₹20 lakh creditors and ₹3 lakh other operating liabilities leaves a ₹42 lakh gap. A purely illustrative 25% borrower margin leaves ₹31.5 lakh as a funding illustration, not a commitment from any lender.

Important bank appraisal considerations

Actual assessments can depend on sales projections, operating cycle, eligible receivables, obsolete inventory, other current assets and liabilities, existing bank finance, promoter funds, margin norms and lender policy. How to use and interpret this calculator

Use the calculator above as a starting point for planning. The notes below explain the assumptions and how to review the result in an Indian MSME financing context.

Operating cycle and finance needs

Net working capital is current assets minus current liabilities. Financing assessments may additionally distinguish operating liabilities, bank borrowing, borrower margin and eligible current assets.

Worked business example

A trading firm has inventory ₹50 lakh, receivables ₹35 lakh, cash ₹5 lakh and operating payables ₹30 lakh. Its simple net working capital is ₹60 lakh. That figure alone does not establish its eligible bank limit.

Checks before relying on the result

Compare turnover assumptions with historical sales and actual receivable days. Investigate unusually old debtors, stock buildup and reliance on short-term borrowing for long-term assets.

What the result means for an MSME

Improving collections or reducing inventory can lower financing needs without increasing credit limits.

Practical review checklist

  • Keep input figures and their period or units consistent.
  • Recalculate using a conservative scenario rather than relying only on an optimistic forecast.
  • Reconcile the assumptions with recent accounts, invoices, bank statements and contractual terms.
  • Discuss product-specific eligibility and methodology with the lender before making a financing decision.
