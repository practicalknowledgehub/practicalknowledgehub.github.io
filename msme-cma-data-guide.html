CMA Data for MSME Bank Loans: Statements, Projections and Checks

Understand the operating statement, balance sheet, working capital assessment and financial projections used in MSME bank credit appraisal.

Educational guide · Published October 2026 · Illustrative examples in Indian rupees

What is CMA data?

CMA (Credit Monitoring Arrangement) data is a structured presentation of historical and projected financial information used in many bank credit assessments. The exact forms, periods and formats depend on the lender, facility and applicable policy. It is not a substitute for audited financial statements or supporting records.

Common CMA components

  • Existing and proposed credit facilities, including limits and outstanding balances.
  • Operating statement: revenue, cost of goods sold, operating expenses, depreciation, interest and profit.
  • Balance sheet: current and non-current assets, liabilities, borrowings and net worth.
  • Current assets and current liabilities analysis for working capital assessment.
  • Fund flow or cash flow projections and key financial ratios.

Illustrative projected operating statement

Assume an MSME manufacturer forecasts the following for Year 1 (₹ lakh). These are hypothetical figures, not prescribed bank norms.

Particulars₹ lakh
Net sales200
Cost of goods sold150
Gross profit50
Operating expenses (excluding depreciation)22
EBITDA28
Depreciation6
EBIT22
Interest expense8
Profit before tax14

Checks: Gross profit = 200 − 150 = ₹50 lakh; EBITDA = 50 − 22 = ₹28 lakh; EBIT = 28 − 6 = ₹22 lakh; PBT = 22 − 8 = ₹14 lakh. Taxes and extraordinary items are omitted for simplicity.

Link the operating statement to the balance sheet

Projected sales should connect to receivables; purchases and production to inventory and trade payables; capital expenditure to fixed assets and borrowings; and retained earnings to net worth. A projection can look profitable yet be inconsistent if these linkages are missing.

For example, projected receivables of ₹40 lakh on ₹200 lakh annual credit sales imply roughly 73 days of collection (40 ÷ 200 × 365), assuming all sales are on credit. Explain why that period is reasonable using past collections and customer terms.

Working capital assessment and ratios

For illustration, projected inventory ₹35 lakh + receivables ₹40 lakh + other operating current assets ₹5 lakh − trade payables ₹20 lakh − other operating current liabilities ₹5 lakh = ₹55 lakh operating working capital gap. This is not automatically the sanctioned cash credit limit: margin, eligibility, exclusions, available bank finance and the bank’s assessment method must be considered.

Current ratio = current assets ÷ current liabilities. Debt-equity and DSCR should be assessed using consistent definitions and relevant financial data.

Checks before submitting CMA data

  • Reconcile opening balances with finalized financial statements.
  • Match sales assumptions to production capacity, orders and market demand.
  • Explain inventory and debtor holding periods.
  • Ensure term-loan repayments and interest flow through projected cash flow.
  • Distinguish GST-inclusive from GST-exclusive figures consistently.
  • Check that the projected balance sheet balances and funding gaps are explained.

Frequently asked questions

Is one CMA format compulsory for every MSME loan?

No. Banks may request different information depending on their internal policy and the nature of the facility.

Can projected profits alone justify a higher limit?

No. Banks also examine operating cycle, actual transactions, repayment record, promoter contribution and overall risk.
