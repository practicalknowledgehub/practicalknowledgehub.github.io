Commercial Property Loan Eligibility in India

Understand commercial property loan eligibility, title checks, valuation, loan-to-value ratio, documents, repayment capacity and financing costs.

What is a commercial property loan?

A business may seek finance to acquire a commercial property, construct business premises, or borrow against an eligible property it already owns. These are different loan purposes with different underwriting requirements. A loan against property (LAP) is not the same as a loan for purchasing commercial premises.

For a broader introduction, read our Loan Against Property for Business guide.

Eligibility checklist

FactorWhat lenders may verify
Property titleOwnership, title chain, encumbrances, approvals and enforceability of mortgage
Property useCommercial use, zoning, occupancy, construction status and approvals
Market valueIndependent valuation and lender-approved loan-to-value limits
Cash flowAudited financials, existing obligations, projected cash flow and DSCR
Borrower profileBusiness vintage, credit history, tax compliance and banking conduct
Loan purposePurchase, construction, expansion or refinance; end-use evidence

Documents typically requested

  • Title deeds, prior conveyances and encumbrance records
  • Building plan approvals, completion or occupancy documents where applicable
  • Property tax receipts and other applicable local approvals
  • Financial statements, income-tax returns, GST information and bank statements
  • Existing loan statements and details of the business purpose
  • Sale agreement, project estimate or quotations where relevant

Costs to budget beyond interest

Consider valuation and legal charges, processing fees, mortgage registration or stamp duty, insurance where applicable, and prepayment terms. Charges vary by lender and state.

Frequently asked questions

Can vacant land be financed?

Eligibility depends on the property's permitted use, lender policy and the specific credit product. Vacant land should not be assumed eligible under all LAP schemes.

Is property value alone enough?

No. Banks also assess legal acceptability, cash flow, credit conduct and loan purpose.

Is the loan amount equal to the property valuation?

No. A lender may apply a margin or LTV cap, and repayment capacity can result in a lower loan amount.
