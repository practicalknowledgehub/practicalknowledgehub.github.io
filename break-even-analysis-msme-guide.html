Break-Even Analysis for MSMEs: How Much Must You Sell?

Break-even analysis estimates the sales level at which operating contribution equals fixed operating costs. It is useful when assessing a new unit, capacity expansion, pricing change or project proposal.

1. Core formulas

Contribution = Sales − Variable Costs.

Contribution Margin Ratio = Contribution ÷ Sales.

Break-Even Sales = Fixed Operating Costs ÷ Contribution Margin Ratio.

2. Worked example

Assume a manufacturing MSME expects annual sales of ₹200 lakh, variable costs of ₹130 lakh and fixed operating costs of ₹42 lakh. The contribution margin is ₹70 lakh, or 35% of sales.

Break-even sales = ₹42 lakh ÷ 0.35 = ₹120 lakh.

The margin of safety is ₹200 lakh − ₹120 lakh = ₹80 lakh, equivalent to 40% of expected sales. At the assumed cost structure, operating profit before interest and tax is ₹28 lakh.

3. What banks should examine

Test the assumptions for raw-material prices, power costs, transport, capacity utilisation and product mix. Classify semi-variable costs carefully. A project may appear profitable at full capacity but struggle during the ramp-up period.

4. Limitations

Break-even analysis assumes stable selling prices, unit variable costs and product mix within the relevant range. It is not a cash-flow forecast and does not automatically include interest, principal repayments, taxes or capital expenditure.

5. Frequently asked questions

Does break-even sales mean the business can repay a loan?

No. A business can cover operating costs but still lack sufficient cash to service debt or fund working capital.

What if variable costs exceed sales?

Then the contribution margin is zero or negative; no positive sales level will cover fixed costs under those assumptions.
