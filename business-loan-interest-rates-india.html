Business Loan Interest Rates in India: How Banks Decide (2026)

Learn how banks price MSME business loans in India: benchmarks, spreads, credit risk, collateral, tenure, fees and an illustrative interest-cost calculation.

Two businesses can borrow from the same bank and receive different interest-rate offers. The difference usually reflects the facility type, bank pricing policy, credit profile, repayment structure and security or guarantee arrangements. This guide explains how to compare offers properly.

Why is there no single MSME loan interest rate?

Interest rates differ across lenders and products, and may change over time. A term loan, cash-credit account, overdraft and government-linked scheme may follow different pricing rules. An advertised starting rate is not a guaranteed rate for every applicant.

How loan pricing generally works

Many floating-rate business facilities use a bank-defined or externally linked benchmark plus a spread, subject to the product’s applicable regulatory framework. The spread can reflect borrower-specific risk, operating costs, product terms and other permitted components. Not all MSME products use the same benchmark.

Illustrative annual rate = 8.00% benchmark + 2.25% spread = 10.25%

This is an educational example, not a current quote from any bank. The actual loan agreement governs repricing, reset frequency and charges.

Factors that can affect the rate

  • Credit history and conduct: overdue accounts, repayment history and existing obligations.
  • Business financials: margins, leverage, debt-service capacity and cash flow volatility.
  • Facility structure: term loan versus revolving cash credit, tenor and repayment profile.
  • Security or guarantee: type and quality of security, or applicable guarantee scheme and its costs.
  • Bank relationship: transaction history and account conduct where relevant to policy.
  • Benchmark movement: variable-rate facilities can reprice under the agreed mechanism.

Worked example: Interest on a cash-credit facility

Suppose a business has a sanctioned cash-credit limit of ₹30 lakh but uses an average outstanding balance of ₹18 lakh for a 30-day period. At an illustrative annual rate of 10.25%, simple interest would be approximately:

₹18,00,000 × 10.25% × 30 ÷ 365 ≈ ₹15,164

Actual bank calculations may use daily products, the precise number of days, rate changes and other contractual terms. Interest is generally tied to utilisation for a revolving facility, while fees may apply separately.

Worked example: Term-loan EMI

For an illustrative ₹20 lakh term loan at 10.25% per annum with monthly reducing balance and 60 equal monthly instalments, the EMI is approximately ₹42,741. The calculation excludes processing fees, insurance, tax and any moratorium or irregular repayment schedule.

Use the sanctioned repayment schedule to compare the true cash-flow burden rather than judging a loan solely by the headline rate.

How to compare two bank offers

  1. Ask both lenders for the written rate, benchmark and spread.
  2. Compare the same amount, tenor, security and repayment schedule.
  3. Include upfront and recurring fees.
  4. Model monthly cash flow under a plausible floating-rate increase.
  5. Review covenants and any collateral or guarantee conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Does Udyam Registration give a fixed concessional rate?

No. Udyam status alone does not establish a universal interest rate.

Is a secured loan always cheaper?

Not necessarily. Pricing depends on the lender, product and full credit assessment.

Can the rate change after sanction?

Yes, for floating-rate facilities according to the agreement and applicable rules.

Where can I find actual bank rates?

Check current official bank rate cards and obtain a written quotation for your proposed facility.
