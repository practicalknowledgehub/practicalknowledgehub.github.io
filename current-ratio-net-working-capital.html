Current Ratio vs Net Working Capital: MSME Credit Analysis Explained

Learn current ratio and net working capital formulas with a practical MSME balance-sheet example and interpretation for bank appraisal.

Educational guide · Published October 2026 · Illustrative examples in Indian rupees

Why these two measures matter

Current ratio is a proportion; net working capital (NWC) is an amount. Both describe aspects of short-term liquidity, but neither independently establishes a borrower’s ability to repay.

Formulas

Current ratio = Current assets ÷ Current liabilities
Net working capital = Current assets − Current liabilities

Use a consistent classification of current assets and liabilities. Some bank assessments use adjusted operating current assets and liabilities, which can differ from the accounting balance sheet.

Worked MSME example

Current balance-sheet item₹ lakh
Inventory30
Trade receivables45
Cash and bank5
Total current assets80
Trade payables25
Short-term borrowings20
Other current liabilities5
Total current liabilities50

Current ratio = 80 ÷ 50 = 1.60 times. NWC = 80 − 50 = ₹30 lakh. Both results are based on the same hypothetical balance sheet.

Why the ratio and NWC can tell different stories

Business A has current assets ₹80 lakh and current liabilities ₹50 lakh: ratio 1.60 and NWC ₹30 lakh. Business B has current assets ₹800 lakh and liabilities ₹700 lakh: ratio about 1.14 and NWC ₹100 lakh. Business B has higher absolute NWC but a thinner relative liquidity cushion.

Neither number tells us whether receivables are overdue, inventory is obsolete or cash is restricted.

What a banker examines beyond the headline ratio

  • Ageing and recoverability of debtors.
  • Inventory quality, valuation and movement.
  • Seasonality and timing of cash collections.
  • Reliance on short-term borrowing for long-term assets.
  • Trends in liquidity rather than a single year-end figure.

Improving working capital quality

Collect receivables faster, avoid excess inventory, match financing tenor to asset life, and build sustainable retained earnings. Reclassifying liabilities merely to improve a reported ratio does not improve actual liquidity.

Frequently asked questions

Is a current ratio of 1.33 mandatory for every MSME?

No universal ratio applies to every bank, facility and borrower. Follow the lender’s current policy and understand the business model.

Can NWC be negative while a business remains operational?

Yes, especially in certain cash-generative models, but it warrants assessment of supplier terms, cash conversion and liquidity risks.
