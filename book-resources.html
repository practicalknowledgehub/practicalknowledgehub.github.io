Capital and government assistance

Government support can reduce the initial funding burden, but assistance is not automatic. First confirm whether the proposed activity is eligible, whether the scheme supports a new or existing enterprise, how much promoter contribution is required, and whether subsidy is released upfront or after specified milestones. Never assume a subsidy will be available as cash for the first payment to a supplier.

Compare government schemes, eligibility and official sources

Project cost and margin

Before approaching a bank, prepare a simple project-cost table covering land or premises, machinery, installation, preliminary expenses and initial working capital. Show separately what you can contribute from savings or other verifiable sources. A higher proposed loan amount does not replace the need to explain your own contribution.

Understand margin money and promoter contribution

Financial projections

Start with quantities you can realistically sell, not the full installed capacity from the first month. Estimate selling price, raw materials, wages, electricity, rent and other operating costs. Test a slower-sales scenario to see whether the business can still meet expenses and repayments.

Prepare realistic sales and profit projections

Working capital

Even a profitable business may run short of cash when customers take time to pay. Estimate the money tied up in stock and receivables, then deduct supplier credit. Revisit these assumptions when sales grow or payment terms change.

Estimate working capital using the calculator

Drawing power

For a cash-credit facility, the sanctioned limit and the amount available to draw can differ. Drawing power depends on eligible stock and receivables, deductions, and the margin specified by the bank. Use actual figures from the latest records rather than sales estimates alone.

Try an indicative drawing-power calculation

Loan repayment

Loan affordability depends on the repayment structure as well as the interest rate. Compare the proposed instalment with cash generated after paying regular operating expenses. Allow for seasonal fluctuations and any moratorium before principal repayment begins.

Explore the EMI calculator

Repayment capacity

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) compares cash available for debt service with the principal and interest obligations for the relevant period. A ratio above one indicates a positive buffer in that calculation, but it is not by itself proof that the business has enough cash on every repayment date.

Understand and calculate DSCR

Bank appraisal

Bankers normally examine the borrower, purpose of finance, business experience, projected cash flows, own contribution, existing liabilities and available security or guarantee cover. Clear, consistent information is more useful than optimistic figures that cannot be explained.
