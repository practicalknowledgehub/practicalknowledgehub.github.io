13-Week Cash Flow Forecast for MSMEs: Weekly Planning Guide

Build a rolling 13-week cash forecast with opening cash, receipts, payments and closing balance.

Updated October 2026 · Independent educational information

A 13-week cash-flow forecast is a short-term liquidity planning tool. It records when cash is actually expected to arrive and leave—not merely when sales and expenses are booked.

Prepare the weekly forecast

  1. Start with actual available bank and cash balances.
  2. List expected customer collections by realistic receipt week, separating overdue debtors.
  3. Enter supplier payments, payroll, GST, rent, loan repayments and capex by due week.
  4. Calculate closing cash each week: opening cash + receipts − payments.
  5. Carry the closing balance into the following week's opening balance and update weekly.

Example

Opening cash ₹3 lakh, expected week-one receipts ₹5 lakh and payments ₹6 lakh give closing cash of ₹2 lakh. If receipts slip by ₹2 lakh, the week-one balance falls to zero. This illustrates why timing matters even for profitable businesses.

Useful stress tests

Model a 15-day collection delay, inventory purchases ahead of season, delayed GST refunds and increased debt instalments. Negative forecast cash is a prompt to consider collection actions, expenditure deferral or an appropriately assessed working-capital facility—not automatic evidence of loan eligibility.
