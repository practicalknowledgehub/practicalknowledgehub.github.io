MSME Margin Money and Own Contribution: What New Entrepreneurs Should Know

For first-time and growing Indian entrepreneurs. The illustrations below are educational and do not represent a bank's approval criteria.

What is own contribution?

Own contribution is the amount an entrepreneur brings into the project from acceptable sources. It may include eligible funds already invested, subject to lender verification. A bank normally expects a borrower to share project risk; the percentage depends on product, security, scheme and appraisal. There is no universal margin percentage for every MSME loan.

Project cost versus means of finance

Suppose a workshop costs ₹40 lakh including machinery, installation and eligible initial expenses. If the lender considers a 25% borrower contribution, the illustrative split is ₹10 lakh from the entrepreneur and ₹30 lakh bank finance. This is an example, not a quoted lending rule. If the borrower has only ₹4 lakh available, the remaining ₹6 lakh is a genuine funding gap that needs an identified, acceptable source.

Why subsidy is not the same as upfront cash

Some schemes provide back-ended margin-money assistance or capital subsidy, subject to approval, lock-in and adjustment conditions. A subsidy application is not an approved disbursement. The entrepreneur may still need to arrange their required contribution and meet other project expenses before any benefit is released. Always read the current scheme rules and lender instructions.

Acceptable evidence and timing

Banks may ask for savings records, proof of asset purchases, capital account entries, source-of-funds explanation and payment trail. Borrowed margin disguised as personal capital can weaken the proposal. Plan when each payment falls due: booking advance, civil work, machinery delivery, installation and working capital buildup.

Ways to reduce a genuine funding gap

Consider implementing the project in phases, negotiating legitimate supplier payment terms, choosing appropriately sized equipment, or bringing in a documented equity partner. Compare project scope with likely sales and debt service. Never inflate project cost to make the required contribution appear lower.
