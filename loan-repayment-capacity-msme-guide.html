Loan Repayment Capacity for MSMEs: DSCR and Cash Flow

Understand cash available for debt service, DSCR and realistic stress testing before taking a business loan.

Updated October 2026 · Independent educational information

A business should assess whether it can service a proposed term loan after operating expenses, tax, maintenance investment and existing commitments. Profit alone is not the same as available cash.

Debt service coverage ratio

For a simplified illustrative check, DSCR = cash available for debt service ÷ (scheduled principal + interest). Lenders may define the numerator and treatment of tax, depreciation and existing debt differently.

Example

Suppose cash available for debt service is ₹18 lakh annually and scheduled principal plus interest is ₹12 lakh. The illustrative DSCR is 1.50. If available cash falls to ₹12 lakh, DSCR becomes 1.00 and the buffer disappears.

Assess repayment under stress

  • Test lower turnover and margins, delayed collections and increased raw-material costs.
  • Include all existing term-loan instalments and relevant interest.
  • Separate temporary working-capital requirements from long-term debt capacity.
  • Compare annual DSCR with the weakest month or quarter for seasonal operations.

Stress-testing debt repayment capacity

Method and assumptions

Forecast operating cash generation after realistic costs, taxes and maintenance expenditure. Compare cash available for debt service against principal and interest payable in each period, including existing loans where relevant.

Illustrative Indian MSME case

If cash available for debt service is ₹40 lakh and annual scheduled principal plus interest is ₹32 lakh, the indicative coverage is 1.25. If sales weakness reduces available cash by 20%, coverage becomes 1.00, leaving no modelled cushion.

Practical implementation

Check year-wise coverage, not just an average. Reconcile forecasts with historical margins, receivable collections, owner withdrawals and the loan repayment schedule. Document the assumptions behind projected improvement.

Documents to keep ready

Maintain recent financial statements, sales and purchase ledgers, debtor and creditor ageing, stock reports, bank account statements and repayment schedules. The information required will vary with the product and lender.

Important limitations

Illustrative calculations are not credit decisions. The lender may adjust eligible assets, cash-flow definitions, margins, security values and repayment assumptions. Verify current terms before applying.

Worked example: a profitable business can still struggle to repay

Consider a small service enterprise expecting ₹24 lakh in annual accounting profit before considering its cash collection pattern. A large part of its sales may remain unpaid at year-end. If only ₹18 lakh of cash is realistically available for scheduled debt service of ₹12 lakh, the illustrative coverage is 1.50—not 2.00 based on accounting profit alone. The precise bank appraisal formula can differ, so this comparison is for understanding cash pressure rather than reproducing a lender's DSCR method.

Stress test three realistic scenarios

Base case₹18 lakh₹12 lakh1.50
Collections delayed₹15 lakh₹12 lakh1.25
Lower sales and higher costs₹12 lakh₹12 lakh1.00

At coverage of 1.00, the model leaves no surplus for unforeseen expenses. A business with seasonal collections should also prepare a monthly cash forecast: an acceptable annual ratio can conceal a month when an instalment falls due before customer payments arrive.

What an entrepreneur should do next

  1. List every existing and proposed loan instalment, including repayment start dates.
  2. Prepare realistic customer collection and supplier payment assumptions.
  3. Keep a downside case with reduced sales or delayed receivables.
  4. Discuss an appropriate repayment schedule with the lender rather than assuming a longer tenure automatically solves a weak business model.
