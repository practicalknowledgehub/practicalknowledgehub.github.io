DSCR Calculation for MSME Business Loans: Formula, Worked Examples and Banker's Perspective

Understand how cash generation is compared with loan repayment obligations, with a practical five-year illustration.

Published 14 October 2026 · Educational guide · All amounts in ₹ lakh unless stated

Cash Conversion Cycle Calculator

Enter annual figures and average working-capital balances in the same unit (for example, all in ₹ lakh). The calculator uses a 365-day year.

Enter your figures and select Calculate.

How the result is calculated

Inventory days = average inventory ÷ annual cost of goods sold × 365. Receivable days = average receivables ÷ annual credit sales × 365. Payable days = average payables ÷ annual credit purchases × 365.

CCC = Inventory Days + Receivable Days − Payable Days.

Read our step-by-step cash conversion cycle guide for interpretation and a worked example.

Illustrative planning tool. Credit-sales and credit-purchases data are preferable to total sales or total purchases where available.

How to use and interpret this calculator

Use the calculator above as a starting point for planning. The notes below explain the assumptions and how to review the result in an Indian MSME financing context.

Working capital locked in operations

Cash conversion cycle (days) = inventory holding days + receivable collection days − payable credit days. A shorter cycle can release cash, although an unusually short inventory cycle may also cause stockouts.

Worked business example

If inventory is held 60 days, customers pay after 45 days and suppliers provide 30 days credit, the cycle is 75 days. A business with ₹1 lakh average daily operating cost may need approximately ₹75 lakh to finance that cycle, before cash reserves and other adjustments.

Checks before relying on the result

Use consistent annual or monthly denominators and cost bases. Verify inventory aging, customer payment behaviour, supplier terms and seasonality rather than relying only on year-end balances.

What the result means for an MSME

A cycle reduction of 10 days may materially reduce borrowing needs. Improvements can come from inventory planning, milestone billing and prompt follow-up of overdue receivables.

Practical review checklist

  • Keep input figures and their period or units consistent.
  • Recalculate using a conservative scenario rather than relying only on an optimistic forecast.
  • Reconcile the assumptions with recent accounts, invoices, bank statements and contractual terms.
  • Discuss product-specific eligibility and methodology with the lender before making a financing decision.
